# WT020 — Hesed, loyal love, kindness

Version 0.3.0; 2026-10-09. Word family. English core with Dutch search aliases.

**Dutch aliases:** hesed, chesed, khesed, trouwe liefde, trouwe goedheid, liefdevolle goedheid, goedertierenheid, verbondsliefde.

**Original terms:** Hebrew חֶסֶד (hesed, also transliterated khesed). Greek renderings must be checked in the actual LXX passage rather than assumed.

**Default explanation:** A full analysis must separate the complete attested range (kindness, mercy, love/loyalty, human devotion), the precise contextual emphasis, and the theological interpretation of the agent. Hesed does not automatically mean every possible sense at once or require an explicit covenant.

**Translation key:** Consider beneficent kindness, loyal/steadfast love, mercy, covenant love when warranted, or retained hesed with a contextual explanation. Dutch: goedheid, trouwe goedheid, trouwe liefde, barmhartigheid, and (in covenant contexts) verbondsliefde. 'Grace/genade' can be a theological connotation, not a universal lexical gloss. Preserve the distinct neighboring words for grace, compassion, covenant and truth.

**Initial orientation:** Exodus 34 names a quality of Yahweh in a covenant-renewal context. Ruth 3:10 applies hesed to human conduct. Micah 6:8 places love of hesed alongside justice and walking humbly.

#### Local reading

Hesed commonly concerns kindness, loyalty, or generous committed concern in a relationship. Exodus 34:6–7 places it within Yahweh's self-description after covenant failure. Its abundance and connection with emet contribute to a portrait of dependable mercy. The surrounding statements about forgiveness and wrongdoing also matter: isolating “loyal love” from the unit can make the passage appear to deny judgment.

Ruth 3:10 uses hesed in Boaz's assessment of Ruth's conduct. Micah 6:8 requires the human hearer to love hesed alongside doing justice and walking humbly with God. These are direct checks against the claim that hesed is only action from a superior to an inferior or only a technical term for God's covenant. Genesis 19:19 concerns kindness shown to Lot, and First Samuel 20:12–17 connects loyal concern with a pledged relationship. A formal covenant may be relevant, but it must be shown in the passage rather than inserted into every use.

The lexical identifiers reveal a further distinction: the Open Scriptures index has separate H2617-linked records for kindness and for shame or reproach. Leviticus 20:17 provides an important disambiguation setting. The fact that the records share spelling and a base Strong number must not make the plugin transfer the kindness sense into a condemnation. The kindness record OSHB:ecp and the reproach record OSHB:eco remain separate in this package.

#### Translation and default explanation

“Kindness,” “loyal love,” “steadfast love,” or a retained hesed with an explanatory gloss may fit different passages. “Covenant loyalty” can be useful when the relation and conduct support it, but it is stronger than a neutral one-word gloss. Dutch *trouwe liefde* or *goedertierenheid* likewise requires an explanation appropriate to the reader and setting.

The default explanation is that hesed often describes reliable or generous kindness expressed within relationships. It is not one fixed legal formula, a term used only of God, or a guarantee that every beneficiary has an identical covenant status. A Greek counterpart must be checked in the particular LXX passage; selecting eleos everywhere from a general lexical description would conceal variation in ancient translation choices.

#### Biblical and canonical development

The useful trajectory connects God's continuing kindness after failure with the kindness expected among his people. Psalm 136's refrain develops the enduring quality of divine hesed through narrated acts. This is a literary and theological pattern, not evidence that the noun always means every act listed in the psalm.

The working synthesis emphasizes dependable relationship expressed in action and reads that pattern alongside justice and mercy. A theology review should ask whether a proposed interpretation identifies the parties and their actual obligations, and whether it allows the text's generosity without making it a contractual wage. Keep the homonym check explicit; this is an example where lexical databases improve accuracy beyond a clean English extract.

**Study questions for the theology check:** Relate divine and human kindness, covenant fidelity, and mercy while allowing each narrative to specify its relationship.


## Full-depth semantic analysis (revised 2026-10-09)

### 1. Lexical possibilities are not cumulative meanings

Ordinary **positive** חֶסֶד / ḥesed (H2617) includes: (a) kindness or beneficent goodness; (b) practical generosity and rescue; (c) mercy and compassionate concern for a vulnerable person; (d) loyal care, relational solidarity, or affection; (e) reliable, steadfast love and commitment, especially within an established relationship; and (f) in appropriate contexts, human fidelity or devotion toward God. These are overlapping, context-sensitive emphases, **not six components guaranteed to appear in every occurrence**. The noun alone does not necessarily mean an explicitly sworn covenant, a superior party’s action, forgiveness, everlasting love, or the later theological definition of grace. Lexical dictionaries list possibilities; the clause and narrative select a use.

The inspected **OSHB:ecp** entry connects to a **partial** Brown–Driver–Briggs record (h.ed.ab) containing human benefits, mercy for vulnerable people, piety toward Yahweh and God's lovingkindness. The attribution is specifically to this inspected selected record, not to all of BDB or uninspected HALOT. Morphological forms such as חַסְדּוֹ (his kindness), חַסְדְּךָ (your kindness) and plural חֲסָדִים (acts of kindness, Gen 32:11) do not themselves fix the context's theological sense.

### 2. Contextual test: participants, situation, action

| Passage | Agent / recipient and situation | Selected emphasis |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Gen 19:19 | Rescuers spare Lot's life | life-saving mercy, generous goodness; no stated prior formal covenant between these participants |
| Gen 21:23 | Abimelech asks Abraham for treatment reciprocal to his own | mutual kindness, reciprocal dealing |
| Gen 24:27 | Yahweh guides Abraham's servant; paired with אֱמֶת / ’emet | God's reliably beneficent care |
| Gen 47:29 | Jacob asks Joseph to bury him in Canaan; hesed + ’emet | family loyalty, care, reliability |
| Josh 2:12–14 | Rahab helps the spies, asks protection for her family | reciprocal kindness, solidarity and a newly pledged obligation |
| Ruth 1:8; 3:10 | Naomi, Ruth and their families | devoted concern, generous kinship kindness |
| 1 Sam 20:14–17; 2 Sam 9:1–3 | Jonathan, David and Jonathan's descendant | promise-keeping / covenant loyalty *enacted* as generous care |
| Exod 34:6–7 | Yahweh discloses His character after covenant betrayal | abundant patient, forgiving, faithful love, alongside justice |
| Deut 7:9 | Yahweh keeps both בְּרִית (berit) and hesed | covenant commitment and goodness, distinct but related |
| Hos 6:4–6 | Israel's fleeting loyalty contrasted with what Yahweh desires | human fidelity to God, inseparable from ethical life |
| Mic 6:8 | God's people must love hesed, alongside justice and humility | practiced and cherished benevolent fidelity |
| Ps 23:6; 136:1–4 | Poetic trust and repeated praise | God's persisting benevolence, steadfast love |

**Human to human:** Josh 2:12 repeats hesed for Rahab's protective action and her request that the spies protect her family; v. 14 promises hesed together with ’emet. A binding promise arises in this encounter, rather than being presupposed before it. Gen 47:29 similarly combines hesed and ’emet in Jacob's request to Joseph. Genuine human kindness can be unconditional in a particular instance, reciprocal in another, and bound by a promise elsewhere; human beings can also fail to sustain it. These differences are contextual, not separate basic definitions for God and humans.

**Divine versus human:** Exod 34:6–7 calls Yahweh “rich in hesed” after Exod 32's covenant violation, while retaining judgment within His self-disclosure. The refrain in Ps 136 is כִּי לְעוֹלָם חַסְדּוֹ (“for His hesed endures forever”). In deliberate contrast, Hos 6:4 says Israel's hesed fades like a morning cloud or dew, before 6:6 declares what God desires. Scripture presents God's reliability as His character; it does **not** follow that “everlasting” is an unavoidable lexical feature of hesed in human speech.

### 3. Overlap with other Hebrew words, without collapse

- **חֵן (ḥen)** = favour/grace and **חַנּוּן (ḥannun)** = gracious. Gen 19:19 places ḥen next to hesed; Exod 34:6 describes Yahweh as ḥannun *and* rich in hesed. God's hesed can therefore *confer grace* or have an undeserved effect, but grace/genade is not the required gloss of every hesed.
- **רַחוּם (raḥum)** = compassionate/merciful. Exod 34:6 names it separately from hesed. Mercy/ontferming/barmhartigheid is nevertheless a valid contextual rendering of hesed where rescue or compassionate help is central.
- **אֱמֶת (’emet)** = truth, reliability, faithfulness. Exod 34:6, Gen 24:27, Gen 47:29 and Josh 2:14 pair it with hesed. A pairing can intensify the whole and still preserve distinguishable terms.
- **בְּרִית (berit)** = covenant. Deut 7:9 pairs it with hesed; “Covenant Love” / *verbondsliefde* is a compelling theological/contextual description of God's hesed *in covenant passages*, not a definition imposed on every occurrence.
- **אַהֲבָה (’ahavah)** = love. Hesed may be loving, but is not interchangeable with every Hebrew word for love. Related חָסִיד (ḥasid, loyal/devout) and verbal forms may contribute wordplay (Ps 18:26), not automatic meaning by shared root.

### 4. Greek renderings: mercy is stronger as a gloss than grace

The checked ancient Greek OT represents hesed with **ἔλεος (eleos, mercy)** in Ruth 3:10, Mic 6:8, Hos 6:6, Greek Ps 135:1 (= Hebrew Ps 136:1), and Exod 34:7; **πολυέλεος (polyeleos, abundant in mercy)** in Exod 34:6; and **δικαιοσύνη (dikaiosynē, righteousness)** in Gen 19:19 and Gen 24:27. The latter reflects particular translation choices, not a universal Hebrew sense. Do not assume a single Greek equivalent throughout.

**Grace / genade** can be a valid *theological connotation* of undeserved loving kindness, but Greek χάρις (charis) and Hebrew ḥen have distinct lexical ranges. **Mercy / barmhartigheid / ontferming** is well attested as a translation equivalent in specific Greek OT passages. Dutch *trouwe goedheid*, *trouwe liefde*, *goedheid*, *barmhartigheid*, *verbondsliefde* and *verbondstrouw* should be chosen by subject, relationship and passage, not by one compulsory slogan.

### 5. Outliers, homonyms and uncertainty

Lev 20:17 has the same written consonants חֶסֶד but means “shame/disgrace” in a prohibited-sexual-act context; the inspected **OSHB:eco** / partial BDB **h.ee.ab** rightly separates it from positive hesed, and the Greek has **ὄνειδος (oneidos, reproach)**. Prov 14:34 is another difficult form often discussed with this rare meaning; it requires independent contextual/textual scrutiny. Isa 40:6 reads חַסְדּוֹ in the received Hebrew while the Greek describes a person's “glory” (δόξα); rare “beauty/splendour” proposals and textual alternatives remain uncertain. Do not retrofit these exceptional settings into the ordinary semantic range, and do not treat a base Strong ID as a homonym decision.

### 6. Bounded canonical/theological synthesis

**Lexical:** hesed has a broad positive relational-goodness range; it need not imply all traits at once. **Contextual:** e.g., Rahab shows reciprocal protecting kindness, Ruth shows family commitment, David shows pledged loyalty, Lot receives merciful rescue, Hosea targets human unfaithfulness, Exodus declares divine goodness after failure. **Canonical:** the same word applies to God and humans; God's enduring reliability derives from His revealed character and promises, while human hesed may be real but unstable. God's covenant love is not reducible to a legal duty; nor does it nullify responsibility or judgment.

**Christological development:** Matt 9:13 and 12:7 *quote* Hos 6:6 as ἔλεος and apply God's demand to compassionate treatment of others. Luke 1:54–55, 72–73 *thematically connects* divine mercy to remembered covenant promises. John 1:14–17's “grace and truth” (χάρις καὶ ἀλήθεια), in a context naming Moses and divine glory, *plausibly echoes* Exod 34's hesed/’emet characterization; John does not use a word-for-word Greek replacement for hesed, and this allusion should not be used as a dictionary definition.

**Methodological rule for every Full word study:** first explain the complete attested lexical range and meaningful exceptional homonyms; second identify the verse-specific meaning and live alternatives from grammar, genre, participants and relationship; third show canonical development and theological implications with confidence labels. Never pack the full word cloud into every verse or derive God's moral constancy from a noun alone.
