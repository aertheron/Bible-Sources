# WT021 — Truth, reliability, faithfulness

Version 0.3.0; 2026-10-09. Word family. English core with Dutch search aliases.

**Dutch aliases:** waarheid, betrouwbaarheid, trouw.

**Original terms:** Hebrew אֱמֶת (emet), אֱמוּנָה (emunah); Greek ἀλήθεια (alētheia), πιστός (pistos). Keep truth and faith vocabulary linked but distinct.

**Default explanation:** Etymological relationships do not make every occurrence a complete theological definition of truth. A suggested Exodus/John link is a canonical argument, not literal identity of the words.

**Translation key:** Decide between truth, reliability, and faithfulness from the actual claim and construction; record the precise term present before making an OT/NT connection.

**Initial orientation:** Exodus 34 places emet beside hesed. Psalm 89 sets divine love and faithfulness within a royal-covenant poem. John 1:14-18 concerns the Word, grace, truth, and the disclosure of God.

#### Local reading

Emet and emunah can concern truth, dependability, fidelity, or firmness according to their construction. Their relationship to trust vocabulary is useful, but deriving every sense from a root associated with firmness would erase actual usage. Greek alētheia and pistos contribute truth and faithfulness language without becoming interchangeable translations in every passage. A truthful statement, a reliable person, and covenant faithfulness are related ideas with different grammatical expressions.

Exodus 34:6–7 pairs hesed and emet within God's self-description. Dependable kindness is an appropriate explanatory emphasis in that setting, while truthfulness should not be excluded simply because a modern explanation prefers relational vocabulary. Psalm 89:1–2 introduces praise of God's loyal love and faithfulness; the wider psalm's treatment of promise and apparent failure gives these qualities a narrative and covenantal weight.

Deuteronomy 32:4 relates God's faithfulness to justice and freedom from wrongdoing. Psalm 119:142 places truth in relation to God's instruction. John 8:31–36 connects remaining in Jesus' word, knowing truth, and freedom. These texts do not oppose truthful content to trustworthy relationship. Romans 3:3–4 explicitly contrasts human unfaithfulness with God's faithfulness and truthfulness, demonstrating how the ideas interact within an argument rather than through a single universal gloss.

#### Translation and default explanation

Choose truth, faithfulness, reliability, or a qualified expression according to the subject and object. “Faithful” describes a person or action differently from “true” describing a statement; a natural target-language translation should preserve that difference. Dutch *waarheid*, *betrouwbaarheid*, and *trouw* similarly require contextual selection. Retain emet or emunah in a note when the shared English vocabulary hides a meaningful distinction.

The default explanation is that biblical truth includes correspondence with what is real and reliable integrity in relevant personal settings. It should not be reduced either to abstract propositions with no relation to conduct or to relationship with no concern for truthful statements. The source construction determines what is asserted locally.

#### Biblical and canonical development

John 1:14–18 describes the Word with grace and truth language. Comparison with Exodus 34 is a valuable canonical proposal because of the larger constellation of glory, divine disclosure, gracious character, and Moses. It is not proof that charis is literally the same lemma as hesed or that every Johannine truth occurrence is a direct citation of emet. Label this connection as a reasoned literary and theological relationship rather than an exact lexical identity.

The working synthesis follows God's dependability, trustworthy revelation, and human life shaped by truth. It should explain how the selected passage joins belief, speech, and conduct. Theology review asks whether an account of faithfulness allows the text's statements about wrongdoing, justice, and promised action to retain their force. The presence of a truth word alone cannot resolve the factual or theological claim made by the surrounding sentence; that claim still requires interpretation and, where relevant, textual comparison.

**Study questions for the theology check:** Trace the reliability of God and His promises and distinguish that trajectory from the meaning of each local term.

### Full-depth semantic audit: truth and reliability

Hebrew **אֱמֶת (*’emet*)** can indicate truth, factual veracity, dependability, faithfulness and reliability depending on clause; **אֱמוּנָה (*’emunah*)** more often draws attention to steadfastness, fidelity or trustworthy support. Greek **ἀλήθεια (*alētheia*)** commonly indicates truth/reality, and **πιστός (*pistos*)** describes faithful or believing persons depending on referent. These overlap but are not synonyms or universal replacements for one another.

**Participants and pairings.** Exod 34:6 pairs Yahweh's *hesed* with *’emet* in a declaration following Israel's unfaithfulness. There “reliability/faithfulness” fits because Yahweh is the characterized speaker and the context is covenant repair; this does not prove *’emet* always means “covenant fidelity.” Psalm 89 connects God's faithfulness with Davidic promise and royal lament, inviting analysis of the addressed God and uncertain human political outcome. John 1:14–18 says Christ is full of grace and truth in a scene recalling Moses and divine disclosure. “Truth” there is not reduced to factual accuracy alone, but the wider revelatory claim comes from the prologue, not from the lexeme.

**Translation and canonical reasoning.** In a testimony setting “truth” may be best; in a promise/character context “faithfulness” or “reliability” may communicate the point. Dutch *waarheid*, *trouw* and *betrouwbaarheid* require the same caution. Opposing “truth” to “love” as if *’emet* denotes cold information ignores the literary collocations. **Limit:** a complete concordance of truth-claims, legal testimony, honesty and enduring faithfulness has not been assembled; some contexts warrant live alternatives.
