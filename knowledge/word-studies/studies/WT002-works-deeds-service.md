# WT002 — Works, deeds, service

Version 0.2.0; 2026-10-05. Word family. English core with Dutch search aliases.

**Dutch aliases:** werken, daden, dienen.

**Original terms:** Hebrew עָבַד (avad), עֲבֹדָה (avodah), מַעֲשֶׂה (maaseh); Greek ἔργον (ergon), ἐργάζομαι (ergazomai). These are several lexical records, not one universal works term.

**Default explanation:** A shared English label does not establish identical objects or arguments. Do not declare James and Paul reconciled merely by changing an English gloss; compare their actual Greek, questions, and reasoning.

**Translation key:** Use work, deed, action, or service according to the activity. Identify works of the law, good works, and an author's examples instead of importing one definition of works everywhere.

**Initial orientation:** Genesis 2:15 concerns work in the garden. Ephesians 2:8-10 distinguishes salvation's source from the good works for which believers are created. James 2 tests a claim to faith through concrete action toward others.

#### Local reading

Work is part of humanity's vocation before it becomes a disputed term in discussions of salvation. Genesis 2:15 presents the human working and keeping the garden. The Hebrew avad can describe agricultural work, service to another person, or service associated with worship. Its occurrence here does not by itself prove that every garden activity is a priestly ceremony. Such a proposal needs the narrative setting and further verbal and thematic correspondences.

Ephesians 2:8–10 distinguishes the source of salvation from its intended result. Salvation is God's gift, excluding boasting in works, while the people addressed are created in Christ for good works. The same broad work vocabulary can describe activity excluded as a ground for boasting and activity positively expected from renewed life. The adjective and the surrounding clauses are therefore essential evidence.

James 2:14–26 begins with someone claiming to have faith while lacking deeds. Its first illustration concerns a poorly clothed, hungry member of the community who receives words without practical help. Abraham and Rahab then supply narrative examples of consequential action. James does not simply offer a dictionary definition of works; he tests whether claimed faith is living and effective. Romans 3:27–31 and 4:1–5 ask different questions about boasting, law, believing, and wages.

#### Translation and default explanation

Translate ergon as work, deed, task, or action according to its object and setting. Keep “works of the law” visible when the Greek construction actually includes law; do not insert that qualification into every occurrence. Conversely, removing the qualification can conceal Paul's specific argument. Dutch *werken*, *daden*, and *dienst* similarly need contextual selection.

The default explanation for Paul and James is that a shared vocabulary does not require an identical question. Paul rejects righteousness treated as a wage earned before God; James rejects a claim to faith that fails to act. This is a contextual reconciliation, not a claim that their Greek words are unrelated. James' statement about justification by works must still be explained in its own terms rather than silently paraphrased away.

#### Biblical and canonical development

A focused trajectory connects created human work, covenant responsibilities, and good works arising from grace. Ephesians gives a particularly useful bridge because grace and good works occur in one literary unit. Galatians 5:6 adds the interaction of faith and love without making every deed salvific merit.

The working conclusion is that action has moral and theological significance, but its significance depends on the relation asserted: ground of boasting, expression of trust, service to a neighbor, or evidence considered in judgment. A theology check should ask which relation a proposed synthesis actually proves. Neither “works never matter” nor “every work earns salvation” follows from the word itself. Keep faith, justification, and grace entries linked so that the user can inspect those relations without requesting a survey of every biblical work passage.

**Study questions for the theology check:** Connect creation's human vocation, covenant obedience, and renewed life. Compare grace, faith, and judgment without making all action equivalent to earning salvation.
