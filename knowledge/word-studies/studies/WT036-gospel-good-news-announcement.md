# WT036 — Gospel, good news, announcement

Version 0.2.0; 2026-10-05. Word family. English core with Dutch search aliases.

**Dutch aliases:** evangelie, goed nieuws, verkondiging.

**Original terms:** Hebrew בָּשַׂר (basar, announce good news); Greek εὐαγγέλιον (euangelion), εὐαγγελίζω (euangelizō). Disambiguate this Hebrew verb from flesh vocabulary.

**Default explanation:** Gospel is not always the name of a written Gospel book. Similar unpointed Hebrew spellings must not merge distinct lexical entries.

**Translation key:** Use good news or gospel with its actual content; explain what is being announced rather than treating the label as self-explanatory.

**Initial orientation:** Isaiah 52:7 announces peace, salvation, and God's reign. Mark 1 joins good news and the kingdom. First Corinthians 15 recalls the proclamation of Christ's death and resurrection.

#### Local reading

Gospel language concerns an announcement whose content and audience must be identified. Hebrew basar is a verb about bringing tidings; it is a separate lexical record from basar meaning flesh despite similar spelling. Greek euangelion and euangelizō name good news and its proclamation in relevant contexts. Their history includes ordinary announcements, but the biblical passage determines what makes its message good news.

Isaiah 52:7 places the messenger's announcement of peace, salvation, and God's reign within restoration language. Isaiah 40:9–11 joins proclamation with God's coming and care. Isaiah 61:1–3 describes good news to the afflicted within a scene involving anointing, release, and comfort. These passages provide scriptural backgrounds for later proclamation; an exact correspondence should be checked in the particular Greek wording rather than inferred from one English “gospel.”

Mark 1:14–15 names God's good news and supplies the announcement about fulfilled time and the nearness of the kingdom, followed by the summons to repent and believe. First Corinthians 15:1–5 recalls the gospel proclaimed and received, then identifies Christ's death, burial, resurrection, and appearances within scriptural fulfillment. Romans 1:1–7 describes the message in relation to God's promise and his Son. The three settings contribute connected content without being identical summaries of every element of Christian teaching.

#### Translation and default explanation

Use good news or gospel as suits the audience, distinguishing the announced message from a written Gospel book. Dutch *goed nieuws* can make the communicative act clear, while *evangelie* preserves familiar terminology. If a user's request concerns “the Gospel of Mark,” that is a literary title question as well as a word question; not every euangelion occurrence means a document.

The default explanation is that the biblical gospel is an announcement of God's saving reign and action through Jesus where the text gives that content. “Good advice,” a general moral philosophy, or a message only about private afterlife arrangements does not adequately represent the selected passages. The noun alone, however, cannot supply every doctrinal detail without the content the author states.

#### Biblical and canonical development

A useful trajectory connects prophetic restoration and royal announcement with Jesus' kingdom proclamation and apostolic testimony to his death and resurrection. Romans 1:16–17 supplies a further explanation of the message's saving significance. Retrieve the anchor that clarifies the current proclamation rather than conducting a survey of all uses.

The working synthesis keeps narrative content, saving significance, and required response together. Theology review asks what the author actually proclaims, to whom, and with what announced result. Claims about imperial announcement customs can enrich the historical context where specifically documented, but resemblance does not prove that every gospel passage directly polemicizes against one emperor or inscription. The scriptural message and its local literary setting remain the primary basis for interpretation.

**Study questions for the theology check:** Trace kingdom announcement and Christ's death and resurrection without reducing the whole proclamation to one modern slogan.
