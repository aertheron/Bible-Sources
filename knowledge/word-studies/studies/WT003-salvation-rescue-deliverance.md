# WT003 — Salvation, rescue, deliverance

Version 0.2.0; 2026-10-05. Word family. English core with Dutch search aliases.

**Dutch aliases:** redding, heil, verlossing.

**Original terms:** Hebrew יָשַׁע (yasha), יְשׁוּעָה (yeshuah); Greek σῴζω (sōzō), σωτηρία (sōtēria). English save can refer to several circumstances.

**Default explanation:** Every occurrence of save is not a technical description of conversion or the afterlife. A healing context and final salvation can be related without being identical uses.

**Translation key:** Translate rescue, save, heal, or salvation as warranted; name what someone is saved from and the passage's time horizon.

**Initial orientation:** Exodus 14 depicts deliverance at the sea. Luke 8:48 addresses a healed woman. Romans 5:9-10 concerns reconciliation and salvation in relation to wrath and Christ's life.

#### Local reading

Salvation vocabulary describes rescue from a danger or condition that the passage must identify. At the sea in Exodus 14:13–14, the immediately visible issue is Israel's deliverance from an approaching army. God's saving action has consequences for Israel's identity and trust, but the word does not first mean an individual's departure to heaven. The narrative supplies the enemy, the threatened people, and the public form of the rescue.

In Luke 8:48, Jesus addresses the woman whose bleeding has stopped. The saving verb can be rendered “made you well” or “saved you,” with a note explaining the healing setting and the restored peace. Insisting that the term refers only to a later theological category would obscure the event being narrated. Insisting that healing excludes all wider saving significance would also outrun the account.

Romans 5:9–10 places salvation in a different argument: justification, reconciliation, Christ's death, and future rescue in relation to wrath and his life. Ephesians 2:1–10 speaks of salvation already given to people previously described as dead in trespasses. Romans 8:18–25 describes an awaited bodily redemption. These temporal perspectives require attention to each clause; they are not competing dictionary senses that automatically contradict one another.

#### Translation and default explanation

Before translating, ask: who saves whom, from what, through what action, and toward what result? “Rescue” can clarify an immediate danger, “heal” can clarify bodily restoration, and “save” can preserve a connection that the author develops. When the requested language uses a church term with a narrow conventional meaning, add the concrete object or a short note rather than assuming the reader understands the full range.

The default explanation is that biblical salvation is broader than one moment of conversion. Its manifestations include deliverance from enemies, restored health, rescue from sin and judgment, and the promised completion of life with God. The text must establish which manifestation is foregrounded. The noun's presence alone does not prove that all conditions, beneficiaries, or mechanisms are identical across passages.

#### Biblical and canonical development

An exodus-to-Christ trajectory is useful where the passage itself concerns deliverance, covenant identity, or liberation from slavery. A second trajectory may connect present renewal with resurrection hope. Select the one that best explains the user's unit; do not automatically retrieve every salvation text.

The working synthesis places salvation in God's initiative to rescue and restore persons and communities, with Christ central to the New Testament's account. A review should check whether a proposed explanation makes future salvation disappear into a past event, or conversely treats present renewal as irrelevant. Questions about final punishment belong in the judgment entry and require their own evidence. “Saved” cannot silently resolve them. Keep justification, healing, redemption, and reconciliation related without treating their different images as interchangeable words.

**Study questions for the theology check:** Develop the exodus-to-Christ trajectory and distinguish present rescue, restored life, and future salvation.
