# WT041 — Son of Man, human figure

Version 0.2.0; 2026-10-05. Name and phrase. English core with Dutch search aliases.

**Dutch aliases:** Mensenzoon, zoon van een mens, menselijke gestalte.

**Original terms:** Aramaic בַּר אֱנָשׁ (bar enash); Greek ὁ υἱὸς τοῦ ἀνθρώπου (ho huios tou anthrōpou); related Hebrew human-description constructions need their own records.

**Default explanation:** Son of Man is not always merely a claim of humanity, and every OT phrase involving a human is not automatically a messianic title.

**Translation key:** Explain an ordinary human description, a vision description, and Jesus' self-designation in their respective settings; avoid substituting one sense automatically.

**Initial orientation:** Daniel 7 contrasts beastly kingdoms with a human-like figure in a heavenly vision. Mark 14 places Jesus' Son of Man saying within His examination and combines scriptural imagery.

#### Local reading

Son of Man language must distinguish ordinary human-description expressions from the distinctive Gospel designation. Aramaic bar enash in Daniel 7:13 identifies a figure like a human being in contrast with the preceding beasts. The vision's movement is toward the Ancient of Days, before whom the figure receives dominion, honor, and a kingdom. It is not simply a scene of the figure descending from heaven to earth; that direction should not be silently reversed by a later eschatological expectation.

Daniel 7:17–27 also interprets the vision in relation to kings and the holy people receiving the kingdom. The relationship between the human-like figure and that people is therefore part of the interpretive question. An individual, representative, or corporate account needs to explain both the vision and its interpretation. The mere presence of son-language cannot decide the issue.

Ezekiel 2:1–5 addresses the prophet as son of man in a setting that distinguishes the commissioned human from God. Its emphasis and grammatical expression differ from Daniel's human-like figure. A connection can illuminate humanity, vulnerability, or representation, but every OT human-description phrase should not be tagged as an explicit prediction of Jesus.

In Mark 2:10, the Son of Man has authority concerning forgiveness; Mark 8:31 speaks of suffering, rejection, death, and resurrection. Mark 14:61–64 joins the designation with sitting at the right hand of Power and coming with heaven's clouds. Psalm 110 and Daniel 7 are important backgrounds to that formulation. The combination explains more than a dictionary gloss of human being.

#### Translation and default explanation

Preserve “Son of Man” as Jesus' recurring designation, supplying a brief explanatory note where the reader might assume it means only a biological relation or ordinary humanity. In Daniel 7, “one like a human being” can make the beast/human contrast clear, while a note keeps the traditional son-of-man expression available for comparison. Dutch *Mensenzoon* and *iemand als een mensenkind* may serve these different settings.

The default explanation is that the Gospel designation brings together authority and human vulnerability in Jesus' own use. Its significance comes from particular sayings and scriptural connections, not from an automatic lexical equation with either a divine title or merely human nature. Exact count claims require a defined corpus and a clear decision about which constructions count.

#### Biblical and canonical development

A focused trajectory connects Daniel's receiving of the kingdom with Jesus' suffering and exaltation. Acts 7:55–56 explicitly uses the designation in Stephen's vision. John 12:34 also records the crowd asking about the Son of Man, so absolute claims that no other Gospel speaker uses the phrase need qualification. A question about the designation is different from an affirmative confession, but both belong in a usage inventory.

The working synthesis reads Jesus' authority and suffering together, explaining the direction and reception of the Danielic scene. Theology review should distinguish a source phrase, a title-like use, an allusion, and a later theological inference. It should also preserve the relation between the representative figure and the people in Daniel's interpretation. Do not derive a complete Christology from the phrase alone or exclude its exaltation significance by translating every occurrence as an ordinary human.

**Study questions for the theology check:** Distinguish Daniel's context, Gospel use, and proposed larger typology; compare their actual images and referents.
