# WT016 — One, alone, I am he

Version 0.2.0; 2026-10-05. Name and phrase. English core with Dutch search aliases.

**Dutch aliases:** één, alleen, Ik ben het.

**Original terms:** Hebrew אֶחָד (echad), אֲנִי הוּא (ani hu); Greek εἷς (heis), ἐγώ εἰμι (egō eimi). A numeral and an identity formula require different records.

**Default explanation:** Echad is not a technical compound-unity word. The words I am do not by themselves establish a divine claim in every occurrence.

**Translation key:** Explain syntactic possibilities for the Shema before choosing one/alone in translation. Identify an ordinary self-identification separately from a proposed scriptural echo.

**Initial orientation:** Deuteronomy 6:4 belongs to Israel's summons to exclusive love and loyalty. Isaiah 43:10 concerns Yahweh's identity and witnesses. John 8:58 places Jesus' statement in an argument involving Abraham.

#### Local reading

Echad is an ordinary Hebrew word for one. Its use can concern one item, a unified group, an exclusive referent, or another contextual construction. It is not a technical theological word meaning “compound unity.” A numeral used of a joined pair or collective does not carry that collective meaning in every occurrence. The composition of what is called one must be established from the subject and clause.

Deuteronomy 6:4–5 places the confession about Yahweh within the summons to love him wholly. Renderings emphasizing “Yahweh is one” or “Yahweh alone” reflect questions about the Hebrew clause and its covenant setting. The practical emphasis on exclusive allegiance is strong; explaining the clause as a mathematical proof for a later theological system overstates the lexical evidence. The following command should remain part of the unit being studied.

Ani hu and egō eimi are identity expressions, not equivalent records for echad. Isaiah 43:10 uses God's self-identification within a proclamation of his uniqueness. John 8:58 uses egō eimi in a striking contrast with Abraham's coming into existence. Its temporal construction and the Gospel's presentation of Jesus matter. John 9:9, however, places the same Greek expression on the formerly blind man's lips as ordinary self-identification. This is a direct check against making every “I am” occurrence a divine claim. John 11:25 explicitly supplies the predicate “the resurrection and the life”; grammatically it is not an absolute, predicate-less I-am saying. Its theological significance should be argued from what Jesus claims in that setting.

#### Translation and default explanation

Preserve one, alone, or the appropriate identity expression with a note where the alternatives materially affect interpretation. Do not translate echad as “a compound unity” or egō eimi as “I am Yahweh” merely to make a theological reading explicit. Explanatory conclusions belong in the commentary, supported by the actual wording and its scriptural connections.

The default explanation is that the words can contribute to a theological argument without independently encoding its whole conclusion. John 10:30's statement about Father and Son being one needs the surrounding claims and grammar; its neuter one does not simply mean that they are one person. First Corinthians 8:4–6 likewise requires its parallel clauses and argument about idols.

#### Biblical and canonical development

A focused trajectory connects Israel's confession of exclusive allegiance with New Testament monotheistic and Christological claims. A separate identity-formula trajectory may connect Isaiah's divine self-identification with relevant Johannine uses. Classify the connection as a possible allusion or interpretive reception unless the passage explicitly cites it; lexical overlap alone is not enough.

The working synthesis should state what the selected texts affirm about God's uniqueness and Jesus' relation to God, then identify the inferential steps. James 2:19 shows that confessing one God is not, by itself, the complete response the letter seeks. Theology review should preserve that ethical context. Neither a “compound unity” gloss nor an automatic dismissal of every Johannine identity formula provides an adequate reading.

**Study questions for the theology check:** Discuss monotheistic confession and Christological reception from the full textual argument, not from an isolated numeral or phrase.
