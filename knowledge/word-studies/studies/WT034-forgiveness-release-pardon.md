# WT034 — Forgiveness, release, pardon

Version 0.2.0; 2026-10-05. Word family. English core with Dutch search aliases.

**Dutch aliases:** vergeving, vrijlating, kwijtschelding.

**Original terms:** Hebrew סָלַח (salach), נָשָׂא (nasa in relevant forgiveness constructions); Greek ἀφίημι (aphiēmi), ἄφεσις (aphesis), χαρίζομαι (charizomai).

**Default explanation:** Release vocabulary does not make debts, sins, captivity, and interpersonal wrongs identical. Explain the specific object before tracing theological links.

**Translation key:** Choose forgive, pardon, release, or remit by construction and object; an ordinary leave/allow use of aphiēmi is not automatically forgiveness.

**Initial orientation:** Exodus 34 concerns forgiveness and accountability. Jeremiah 31 places forgiveness within the new-covenant promise. Mark 2 concerns Jesus' authority to forgive. Ephesians 1 relates forgiveness to redemption.

#### Local reading

Forgiveness vocabulary identifies release or pardon in relation to a particular wrong, debt, or liability. Salach concerns pardoning in relevant Hebrew uses. Nasa ordinarily has a much wider lifting, carrying, or taking range and participates in forgiveness constructions when its object and setting support them. Greek aphiēmi can concern letting go, leaving, or forgiving; aphesis concerns release or remission; charizomai can concern gracious giving or forgiving.

Exodus 34:6–7 describes God's bearing or forgiving wrongdoing while retaining accountability in the surrounding unit. Leviticus 16:20–22 presents a ritual bearing away of confessed wrongdoing. Similar English language can represent different constructions and images; the plugin must identify them rather than assume that every “bear” is the same mechanism of pardon.

Jeremiah 31:34 places forgiveness and the non-remembrance of sin within the new-covenant promise. The statement concerns God's treatment of the people's wrongdoing, not a claim that God has literally lost factual knowledge. Mark 2:1–12 places Jesus' declaration of forgiveness within a challenge about authority and a healing sign. Its force derives from the narrative's question and response rather than from the forgiveness verb alone.

#### Translation and default explanation

Use forgive, pardon, remit, release, or leave according to the object and construction. Dutch *vergeven*, *kwijtschelden*, and *vrijlaten* may distinguish sin, debt, and captivity settings. For aphiēmi, identify whether the object is sin, a financial debt, a person, or something left behind; an unqualified “forgive” is not appropriate for every occurrence.

The default explanation is that forgiveness removes or relinquishes the wrong's liability in the relationship described. It does not automatically erase every consequence, restore immediate trust, or establish that no protection or accountability is needed. Matthew 18:21–35 compares forgiveness with a released debt in a parable; the financial image explains an aspect of the relation without making all wrongs identical financial transactions.

#### Biblical and canonical development

Luke 4:16–21 uses release language within Jesus' reading and announcement from Isaiah. Ephesians 1:7 connects redemption and forgiveness with Christ's blood. Colossians 3:12–14 places forgiving others within communal life shaped by God's action. These supply focused trajectories of liberation and renewed relationships, but a thematic connection does not make release from captivity and forgiveness of sins one lexical sense in every occurrence.

The working synthesis joins God's pardon with restored possibility for faithful relationship and human forgiveness patterned on that gift. Theology review should ask what is released, whose authority is involved, and what the passage says about the response. It should avoid adding unstated requirements or removing stated ones merely to fit a universal formula. In interpersonal application, distinguish pardon, reconciliation, restored access, and trust: the lexical presence of forgiving language does not independently settle all four. Keep atonement and repentance linked where the unit itself supplies the relation.

**Study questions for the theology check:** Follow divine pardon, renewed relationship, and Jesus' authority without assuming forgiveness abolishes every temporal consequence.
