# WT001 — Faith, belief, trust

Version 0.2.0; 2026-10-05. Word family. English core with Dutch search aliases.

**Dutch aliases:** geloof, geloven, vertrouwen.

**Original terms:** Hebrew אָמַן (aman, including the causative believing construction); Greek πίστις (pistis), πιστεύω (pisteuō), πιστός (pistos). Keep noun, verb, and adjective distinct.

**Default explanation:** Faith is not automatically mere agreement with a proposition. Conversely, translating every occurrence of pistis as allegiance or faithfulness predetermines disputed passages.

**Translation key:** Choose believe, trust, faith, faithfulness, or faithful from the actual form, object, and argument; they are not interchangeable in every sentence.

**Initial orientation:** Genesis 15:6 concerns Abraham's response to God's promise. Romans 4 contrasts believing with a wage earned by work. Galatians 5:6 places faith within a discussion of circumcision and love.

#### Local reading

Faith language identifies a response, a quality of reliability, or what is believed. In Genesis 15:6, Abraham believes Yahweh in the setting of the promised offspring. The Hebrew verb is from aman in a believing construction; the root's other uses, such as supporting or being firm, do not all become meanings of this occurrence. Romans 4:1–5 reads this narrative through the contrast between a wage owed to a worker and righteousness credited to someone who trusts God. Faith's importance here lies in its object and its relation to God's promise, rather than in the intensity of an inward feeling.

Greek pistis has several contextual uses. Romans 3:3 concerns God's faithfulness despite human unfaithfulness. Matthew 23:23 places pistis beside justice and mercy, making faithfulness a useful translation candidate. Other passages concern believing or trusting, and still others use “the faith” for the message or commitment shared by a community. The adjective pistos may describe someone reliable or someone believing. Identifying its referent and construction matters more than imposing one English label on the family.

#### Translation and default explanation

For Genesis 15:6, “Abraham trusted Yahweh” makes the personal object clear; “believed Yahweh” also works. Dutch *vertrouwde op* and *geloofde* require equivalent attention to their objects. Preserve the distinction between pistis, pisteuō, and pistos in interlinear records. A translation key should explain when faithfulness, trust, belief, or the content of faith is intended.

The constructions commonly called “faith of Christ” in Romans 3:22 and Galatians 2:16 need a separate note. Reading them as faith in Christ or as Christ's faithfulness involves syntax and the author's argument; the genitive ending alone does not decide the question. Both should be represented as supported interpretive candidates where unresolved. The wider passage's explicit believing language remains relevant whichever account is preferred. “Allegiance” can illuminate committed trust in some settings, but it is an interpretive emphasis rather than a universal replacement for pistis.

#### Biblical and canonical development

The useful trajectory is promise received in trust, God's reliability through covenant failure, and faith expressed in a renewed way of living. Galatians 5:6 connects faith with love without turning love into a lexical definition of faith. Romans 4 excludes boasting in an earned wage; James 2 challenges a profession that leaves a needy neighbor unaided. Those arguments should be compared before declaring either author indifferent to conduct.

The working synthesis is that biblical faith normally involves trusting a trustworthy God and responding to the promise or message at issue. It cannot be reduced to bare factual agreement, but every occurrence also cannot be expanded into an entire theory of obedience. A theology review asks whether a proposed translation preserves God's initiative, the stated human response, and the argument's distinction between gift and achievement.

**Study questions for the theology check:** Trace promise, trust, and fidelity while preserving the distinction between God's reliability and the human response. Review disputed genitive constructions individually.
