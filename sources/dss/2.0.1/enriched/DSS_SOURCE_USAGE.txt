DSS SOURCE USE FOR THE BIBLE STUDY PLUGIN

Retrieve an exact passage per source scroll node. Use the explicit critical
annotations, which retain reconstruction and uncertainty across bracket
boundaries. Read the supplied edition's Unicode transcription and original
encoding together where a disputed detail matters. Keep fragment/line references.

Quote reconstructed letters as editorial reconstructions. Separate unmarked
letters, uncertainty, missing-sign tokens, corrections, removals, and alternatives.
Unmarked means no relevant TF flag; it is not an independent ink verification.
Counts describe the supplied slots; never turn them into a percentage of how
much of an entire verse survives. Do not fill gaps from MT/LXX without labelling it.

For grammar, retrieve the linguistic schema, raw source tag, source lexeme,
converted source features, and any derived analysis. Lexeme pointing and parsing
are linguistic analyses, including where the surface text is reconstructed.
Original morpho is Abegg's tag; source decompositions and BHSA additions are
distinct. Preserve both analyses when they differ and explain their tag systems.
Do not claim a derived model was independently verified or that a pipeline-level
correction statement proves manual review of this token. Inspect merr if present.
Vocabulary and roots support contextual interpretation; they do not generate it.

Source biblical flags describe source classification. Non-biblical material can
quote scripture, and verse mapping does not by itself establish manuscript genre.
Unmapped fragments keep their physical identities. Repeated manuscript labels
may be multiple TF source groups; do not count them as independent corroboration.

Use manuscript catalogues, editions, images, and primary scholarship for dating,
genre, palaeography, or arguments preferring a reading. These exports do not supply
those judgements. Cite the particular witness and distinguish its transcription,
editorial reconstruction, linguistic analysis, and the study's own inference.

Proceed from textual evidence to translation and contextual exegesis, then biblical
development and canonical synthesis. A theological preference cannot establish
a manuscript reading. Explain material alternatives and the limits of the evidence.
